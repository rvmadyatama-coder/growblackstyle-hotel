Understanding stock-market movements

Stock prices reflect expectations about future cash flows, risk and the return investors require. A company can report higher earnings while its share price falls if results disappoint relative to expectations or the outlook weakens. Separate a company's operating performance from the valuation investors are willing to pay.

Useful dimensions for research

  • Company fundamentals: revenue, margins, cash flow, debt, capital spending and management guidance.
  • Valuation: compare ratios only across businesses with suitable accounting, growth and risk characteristics.
  • Rates and inflation: examine financing costs and discount-rate assumptions without treating them as the only drivers.
  • Market structure: liquidity, spreads, index flows and concentration can affect short-term price behaviour.

A disciplined comparison

Use the same reporting period and currency when comparing companies. Identify whether figures are reported, adjusted, trailing or forward-looking. Read original company filings and exchange disclosures, and label analyst estimates as estimates. Past performance does not establish future results; shares can lose value, including the full amount invested.
