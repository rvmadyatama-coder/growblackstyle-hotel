Reading economic indicators

Economic indicators are measurements with defined populations, periods, units and revision schedules. A headline number is incomplete without knowing whether it is seasonally adjusted, nominal or real, monthly or annual, and preliminary or revised.

A practical reading checklist

  • Identify the statistical agency, release date and reference period.
  • Read the definition and methodology, not only the headline figure.
  • Compare like-for-like periods and note seasonal adjustment.
  • Separate the observed result from market expectations and subsequent interpretation.
  • Check revisions and related indicators before drawing a broad conclusion.

From data to market interpretation

Markets react to the difference between new information and expectations, as well as to the likely policy response. One release rarely explains an entire market move. Use official statistical agencies and central-bank publications as primary sources, state uncertainty clearly and avoid presenting association as proof of causation.
