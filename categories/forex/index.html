Understanding the foreign-exchange market

The foreign-exchange market prices one currency against another. A quote such as EUR/USD expresses how many U.S. dollars are needed for one euro; reversing the pair changes the interpretation. Always identify the base currency, quote currency, quote convention and time of the observation before comparing prices.

What readers should examine

  • Interest-rate expectations: compare central-bank decisions with what markets had already priced in.
  • Inflation and economic releases: note the release date, country, measure and revision status.
  • Trade and capital flows: distinguish goods-and-services flows from portfolio and direct investment flows.
  • Liquidity and execution: spreads, market hours, order size and volatility affect the price actually received.

How to read a currency move

Record the currency pair, direction, time zone, data source and comparison window. A currency move occurring after an announcement does not by itself prove that the announcement caused it. Other markets, positioning and simultaneous news may also matter. Leveraged FX products can magnify losses as well as gains.
